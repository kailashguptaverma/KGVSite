Overview

During summer 2026, I worked as a Robotics Hardware Intern at Johnson & Johnson MedTech on the OTTAVA surgical robotics program. My work focused on thermal engineering: using computational analysis and physical tests to understand how hardware layout influenced temperature and airflow.

I used SolidWorks Flow Simulation for CFD, worked through numerous physical layout tests, and quantified their behavior using recorded test data. The internship gave me experience moving between a digital model and a physical setup, then using measurements to evaluate the design choices in between.

03

Design & development

Thermal analysis and testing

  1. CFD analysis

    SolidWorks Flow Simulation helped me investigate how air moved through a layout and how that flow related to component temperatures. The models provided a way to explore design options and develop explanations for the thermal behavior I was seeing.

  2. Layout testing

    I worked through numerous layout tests to examine the hardware in a physical setup. This made packaging and the arrangement of components part of the thermal investigation, alongside the simulation work.

  3. Data acquisition

    I used recorded data-acquisition results to compare thermal behavior across the tested configurations. The measurements made it possible to evaluate differences quantitatively instead of relying only on qualitative observations.

  4. Model and test comparison

    Comparing the model with physical results helped me think critically about both. A simulation depends on how the system is represented, while a physical test depends on its setup and measurement conditions. Working with both made the reasoning behind each design decision more important.

GD&T training

Alongside the project work, I took a GD&T class. It helped me better understand how drawings communicate geometry, allowable variation, and functional relationships between parts. That training broadened my view of hardware development beyond nominal CAD geometry.

OTTAVA system context

OTTAVA is Johnson & Johnson MedTech’s robotic surgical system for soft-tissue surgery. Its architecture incorporates four robotic arms into the operating table, rather than placing them on separate carts or an overhead boom. J&J describes synchronized movement of the table and arms as “twin motion,” supporting patient repositioning during a procedure.

That larger system provides the context for my internship. My individual work focused on thermal analysis and hardware testing within the program.
