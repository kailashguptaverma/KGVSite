Overview

A note on project details: Much of my work is confidential, so I can’t share specific designs, prototype photos, calculations, or internal results. This page focuses on the engineering methods I used and the skills I developed.

Fulfil builds robotic systems for online grocery fulfillment. Its public platform combines inventory storage, automated dispensing, mobile ShopBot robots, and temperature-controlled areas to pick and pack orders directly into grocery bags.

During summer 2025, I worked on mechanical hardware supporting bag handling and tote positioning. These tasks sit at the interface between flexible packaging and repeatable automation: the hardware has to accommodate variation while fitting into a larger moving system.

03

Design & development

Compliance and force control

A compliant mechanism allows motion under load instead of forcing every part to meet at one exact position. I used spring calculations to connect available travel with contact force, then checked whether the mechanism could accommodate variation without overstressing the material or damaging what it handled.

General engineering principles
SPRING RESPONSE
F = kδ

For an ideal linear spring, stiffness k sets how quickly force rises with deflection δ. More travel can improve tolerance accommodation, but it also changes force and packaging requirements.

TOLERANCE ACCUMULATION

Worst-case stackups consider the combined dimensional extremes. Root-sum-square estimates can help assess independent random variation when the statistical assumptions are justified. I used tolerance analysis to connect dimensional uncertainty to the motion a mechanism needed to accommodate.

These are general principles, not Fulfil dimensions, design calculations, or performance data.

Stress and material selection

I compared spring behavior with material strength and manufacturing constraints. The tradeoff was broader than stiffness alone: useful travel, bending stress, available space, fabrication methods, and assembly all affected whether a concept was practical.

Positioning and actuation

For the positioning work, I compared linkage and actuator approaches while considering load paths, alignment, packaging, and behavior when power was removed. I coordinated those mechanical choices with the electrical and testing work rather than treating actuator selection as a standalone task.

Testing and manufacturability

Prototype evaluation helped identify contact, clearance, and assembly issues that were difficult to resolve from calculations alone. I used those observations to revise the design and consider machining, molding, component sourcing, and cost.

Results & next steps

I delivered concept comparisons, CAD, calculation work, prototype evaluations, and a technical handoff. Further system testing and production-readiness work remained at the end of the internship.

The summer strengthened my ability to turn an open-ended hardware problem into requirements, analysis, and testable decisions—and to explain the tradeoffs clearly to the next engineer.
